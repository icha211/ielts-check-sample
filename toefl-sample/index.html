TOEFL ITP 101

The TOEFL ITP (Level 1) evaluates the English proficiency of non-native speakers in academic contexts. It is a 115-minute, multiple-choice exam consisting of 140 questions.

1. Test Structure and Format

The test is entirely paper-based and does not include speaking or essay-writing components. It is divided into three distinct, strictly timed sections:

SectionFocusTime LimitQuestionsScore Range
1. Listening ComprehensionSpoken academic English35 minutes5031-68
2. Structure & Written ExpressionGrammar and syntax25 minutes4031-68
3. Reading ComprehensionAcademic reading material55 minutes5031-67
TotalAll sections115 minutes140310-677

Section Breakdown

  • Listening Comprehension: Tests your ability to understand spoken English in college settings. It features short conversations between two people (30 questions), longer conversations (8 questions), and short academic lectures (12 questions).
  • Structure and Written Expression: Tests your recognition of correct grammar and sentence structure. It includes 15 sentence-completion questions and 25 error-identification questions.
  • Reading Comprehension: Tests your ability to read and understand academic texts, including main ideas, specific details, vocabulary in context, and implied meanings.

Sentence-completion example: The American bald eagle, ____________ on the endangered species list, has made a remarkable recovery in recent decades.

  • (A) which once
  • (B) once placed
  • (C) it was once
  • (D) was once

Error-identification example: The [A] rapidly development [B] of solar technology has [C] made renewable energy more [D] accessible than ever before.

  • (A) rapidly
  • (B) of
  • (C) made
  • (D) accessible

2. How the TOEFL ITP is Scored

Because it is a multiple-choice test, you are graded based on the number of correct answers, with no penalty for guessing. The absolute lowest score you can receive is 310, and the highest is 677.

The Scoring Formula

  1. Add the three scaled section scores together.
  2. Divide the sum by 3.
  3. Multiply that number by 10.

Calculation Example: If you score 54 in Listening, 53 in Structure, and 56 in Reading: 54 + 53 + 56 = 163; 163 divided by 3 = 54.33; 54.33 multiplied by 10 = 543 (Total Score).

3. CEFR Level Mapping

Educational Testing Service (ETS) maps TOEFL ITP scores to the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). Most institutions require a B1 or B2 level for graduation or academic placement.

CEFR LevelEnglish ProficiencyTOEFL ITP Total Score Range
C1Advanced627-677
B2Upper-Intermediate543-626
B1Intermediate460-542
A2Waystage (Basic)337-459

Scores below 337 fall under the A2 threshold, and the test does not measure C2 level proficiency.

4. TOEFL ITP vs. TOEFL iBT: What's the Difference?

While both exams are created by ETS and measure academic English, they serve entirely different purposes.

FeatureTOEFL ITPTOEFL iBT
Primary UseInternal university placement, exit exams, local requirements.International university admissions, visas, scholarships.
FormatPaper-based (Scantron).Internet-based (Computer).
Skills TestedListening, Grammar (Structure), Reading.Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing.
Test DurationAbout 2 hours.About 2 hours.
Scoring310 to 677.0 to 120.
PriceGenerally lower, set by the local institution.Higher, standardized globally.

5. Quick Preparation Tips

  1. Target the Structure Section: Because all three sections carry equal weight in the final score, improving your grammar is often the fastest way to boost your total. A single point improvement in a section scales up to over three points on your final score.
  2. Never Leave Blanks: There is no negative marking for incorrect answers. If you are running out of time, guess.
  3. Pace Yourself in Reading: You have 55 minutes for 50 questions, roughly one minute per question including reading dense academic passages. Practice skimming for main ideas before reading the detailed questions.
  4. Focus on the Second Speaker: In the Listening short conversations (Part A), the answer to the question is almost always found in what the second speaker says.
